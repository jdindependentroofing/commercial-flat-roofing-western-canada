<p>If your commercial roof is leaking, act in this order: protect people and inventory, contain the water, photograph everything, stop any further damage you can safely stop from inside the building, and then book a licensed contractor for <a href="https://www.jdindependentroofing.ca" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow">commercial roof repair Edmonton</a> property owners can rely on. Do not climb onto the roof yourself, especially in snow, ice or wind, and do not seal the leak with caulk or tar before a professional finds the actual entry point. Water usually travels a long way from where it enters the membrane to where it drips into the building, so a quick patch at the visible stain rarely fixes the problem.</p>
<p>The steps below explain what to do in the first hour, the first day and the first week, so you reduce damage, protect your insurance claim and give the roofer what they need to diagnose the problem quickly.</p>
<h2 id="what-should-you-do-first-when-a-commercial-roof-starts-leaking">What Should You Do First When a Commercial Roof Starts Leaking?</h2>
<p>Start with safety, then containment. Water near light fixtures, electrical panels, server rooms or ceiling-mounted equipment is a hazard, so shut off power to the affected area if you can do it safely, and call your electrician if you cannot.</p>
<ul>
<li>Move people away from sagging ceiling tiles or bulging drywall, which can fail suddenly under the weight of trapped water.</li>
<li>Cover or relocate inventory, files, computers and machinery.</li>
<li>Place buckets or tarps under active drips and poke a small hole in a bulging ceiling tile or panel so water drains into a bucket rather than collapsing the ceiling.</li>
<li>Use wet vacuums or mops to keep water off the floor and prevent slip injuries.</li>
<li>Alert tenants, staff and your property manager so everyone knows which areas to avoid.</li>
</ul>
<p>These steps do not fix the roof. They limit the damage while you arrange for a proper repair.</p>
<h2 id="how-do-you-document-a-roof-leak-for-insurance-and-the-roofer">How Do You Document a Roof Leak for Insurance and the Roofer?</h2>
<p>Documentation protects your insurance claim and speeds up the diagnosis. Insurers generally want evidence of when the damage appeared, how it spread and what you did to limit it.</p>
<ul>
<li>Take dated photos and video of ceiling stains, drips, wet insulation, damaged equipment and standing water.</li>
<li>Note the date and time the leak started, weather conditions at that time and whether it leaks only during rain, snowmelt or wind.</li>
<li>Mark the edge of each ceiling stain with a pencil and date it, so the roofer can see whether the leak is growing.</li>
<li>Keep receipts for emergency cleanup, temporary protection and any damaged items.</li>
<li>Review your policy and report the loss to your broker or insurer promptly, because many policies have notice requirements.</li>
</ul>
<p>Also gather your roof's history if you have it: installation date, previous repairs, warranty paperwork and any past inspection reports.</p>
<h2 id="why-is-the-leak-not-always-where-the-water-appears">Why Is the Leak Not Always Where the Water Appears?</h2>
<p>Water rarely drips straight down from the hole. It enters through a defect in the membrane, flashing or seam, then follows insulation, decking, steel flutes and beams until it finds a gap and appears inside.</p>
<p>That is why a stain over one tenant's space can come from a failed seam or drain several metres away. On flat roofs, the most common entry points are:</p>
<ul>
<li>Failed seams or laps in TPO, EPDM or modified bitumen membranes</li>
<li>Cracked or lifting flashing at parapet walls, curbs and roof edges</li>
<li>Penetrations around vents, pipes, HVAC units and skylights</li>
<li>Clogged or poorly sloped drains that cause ponding water</li>
<li>Blistering, splitting or punctures from foot traffic or fallen debris</li>
</ul>
<p>Trying to seal the visible spot from above without a proper inspection often wastes money and traps moisture under the membrane.</p>
<h2 id="is-this-an-emergency-commercial-roof-repair-or-can-it-wait">Is This an Emergency Commercial Roof Repair or Can It Wait?</h2>
<p>Treat it as an emergency if any of these apply: water is entering near electrical systems, the ceiling is sagging or visibly holding water, the leak is active and heavy, stored goods or equipment are at risk, or the leak affects a space where you cannot operate safely.</p>
<p>A slow, intermittent drip that appears only after heavy rain may allow you to schedule a repair within days rather than hours. Even then, waiting weeks is costly. Moisture in insulation reduces its performance, promotes mould and can corrode the steel deck, so a small leak often turns into a much larger replacement scope.</p>
<p>In Edmonton, timing matters more than in milder climates. Water that enters during a thaw can refreeze, expand in cracks and split seams further, so a leak that starts during a chinook can be worse after the next cold snap.</p>
<h2 id="what-should-you-not-do-before-calling-a-commercial-roofing-contractor-in-edmonton">What Should You Not Do Before Calling a Commercial Roofing Contractor in Edmonton?</h2>
<p>Some well-meant fixes make the repair harder or void your warranty.</p>
<ul>
<li>Do not go up on the roof in winter, in wet conditions or without fall protection.</li>
<li>Do not apply roofing cement, silicone or caulk to the first crack you see, since it can hide the real defect and prevent proper welding or adhesion of a repair.</li>
<li>Do not chip ice or shovel snow with metal tools that can puncture the membrane.</li>
<li>Do not ignore the leak until the next big storm.</li>
<li>Do not hire the first person who knocks on your door after a storm, and always ask for proof of licensing, insurance and WCB coverage.</li>
</ul>
<p>If your roof is still under a manufacturer or installer warranty, call the warrantor before authorizing work, because unapproved repairs can affect coverage.</p>
<h2 id="what-does-a-professional-commercial-roof-inspection-involve">What Does a Professional Commercial Roof Inspection Involve?</h2>
<p>A proper inspection finds the source of the leak and checks the condition of the surrounding roof so you can decide between repair and replacement.</p>
<p>A qualified roofer will typically:</p>
<ul>
<li>Inspect the membrane, seams, flashing, drains, parapets and penetrations for visible defects</li>
<li>Check the underside of the deck and ceiling for staining patterns that point to the entry location</li>
<li>Test for trapped moisture in the insulation using infrared scanning or a moisture survey when the leak source is not obvious</li>
<li>Assess drainage and slope to see whether ponding water is contributing</li>
<li>Provide photos, a written scope and a quote that separates urgent repairs from recommended maintenance</li>
</ul>
<p>Ask the contractor to explain whether a localized repair is enough or whether the roof is nearing the end of its service life.</p>
<h2 id="how-do-you-choose-the-right-roofing-contractor-for-a-commercial-roof-leak">How Do You Choose the Right Roofing Contractor for a Commercial Roof Leak?</h2>
<p>Choose a contractor with commercial flat roof experience, not just residential shingle work, because membrane systems need different materials, tools and welding or adhesion methods.</p>
<p>Before you hire, confirm:</p>
<ul>
<li>They hold commercial liability insurance and WCB coverage</li>
<li>They have worked on your roof type, such as TPO, EPDM, modified bitumen or built-up roofing</li>
<li>They offer written estimates with a clear scope and workmanship warranty</li>
<li>They can respond quickly for emergency tarping and temporary protection</li>
<li>They provide references from other Edmonton commercial properties</li>
</ul>
<p>A good contractor will explain the cause of the leak in plain language, show you photos of the defect and tell you what the repair will and will not solve.</p>
<h2 id="what-questions-should-you-ask-when-calling-a-roofer-about-a-leak">What Questions Should You Ask When Calling a Roofer About a Leak?</h2>
<p>Have this information ready when you call, because it speeds up scheduling and quoting:</p>
<ul>
<li>Roof type and approximate age</li>
<li>Location of the leak inside the building and when it started</li>
<li>Whether it leaks only in rain, during snowmelt or all the time</li>
<li>Any past repairs or active warranties</li>
<li>Whether any electrical, inventory or operations are at risk right now</li>
</ul>
<p>Ask how soon they can attend, whether they can provide temporary protection, how the quote is structured and what documentation they will give you for your insurer.</p>
<h2 id="what-happens-after-the-immediate-leak-is-controlled">What Happens After the Immediate Leak Is Controlled?</h2>
<p>Once the leak is stopped, plan the next steps so it does not return. Ask for a written report, schedule repairs for any secondary defects the inspection found, dry out wet insulation and ceiling materials, and consider a maintenance program with inspections each spring and fall. Regular checks of drains, flashing and seams are cheaper than emergency calls, and they help you spot problems before the next freeze-thaw cycle.</p>
